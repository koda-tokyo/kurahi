# Kurahi 2.5.0

日本語: [Kurahi 2.5.0](/ja/releases/2.5.0)

Kurahi 2.5.0 brings **Super SSO**: one company sign-in for every surface, passkeys built in, and a
fresh authentication for every important decision, recorded in the signed evidence. Kurahi is
built by the KODA SōKEN team. The complete list of changes is in the
[changelog](https://github.com/koda-tokyo/kurahi/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md).

With 2.5.0 you can:

- **Sign in with passkeys, with or without an identity provider.** Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello
  or a security key; an authenticator app as an alternative. Free in every plan.
- **Sign every important decision with a fresh authentication.** Approvals, raising autopilot
  levels, lifting the emergency stop, identity administration and data governance actions ask the
  person to confirm it is them; the evidence records when and how they authenticated.
- **Use the same company sign-in on the command line and in Kurahi Desktop.** `kurahi login`
  shows a code the person confirms in the browser; Desktop signs in through the system browser.
  Each device has its own session that can be revoked on its own.

See [Enterprise identity](/security/identity) for configuration and details.

## What is new

- **Passkeys and one-time codes** (`gateway.identity.passkeys`): WebAuthn (ES256, RS256, EdDSA;
  user verification required by default) and TOTP (encrypted at rest). An identity admin's one-time
  link enrols a person's first authenticator; people add further devices themselves after a
  step-up.
- **Step-up for decisions** (`gateway.identity.stepUp`): on by default when identity is configured,
  with `maxAgeSeconds` 300. Re-authentication at the identity provider (OIDC `max_age=0` and
  `prompt=login`, SAML `ForceAuthn`) or with a passkey or one-time code; it never switches persons.
  Stopping, demoting, revoking, sending back and cancelling stay immediate.
- **Authentication in the evidence:** sessions record OIDC `auth_time`, `amr` and `acr` or SAML
  `AuthnInstant` and `AuthnContextClassRef`; each covered decision records them as `authn`.
- **Command line and desktop sign-in:** `kurahi login`, `logout` and `whoami` (device authorization
  grant, RFC 8628) and native apps with loopback redirects and PKCE (RFC 8252).

## Upgrade from 2.4.0

`kurahi update` (or `install.sh` / `install.ps1` with `--version 2.5.0`) installs 2.5.0 as usual;
see [Updating](/install/updating).

**If you use SSO, step-up is on after the upgrade.** People who approve, raise autopilot levels,
lift the emergency stop or administer identity are asked to authenticate again when their last
authentication is older than 5 minutes. Sessions from 2.4.0 have no recorded authentication and
are asked at the first covered decision. To adjust:

- raise `gateway.identity.stepUp.maxAgeSeconds` (up to 3600);
- limit approvals to larger amounts with `actions.approvals: { amountAtLeast: … }`;
- or set `gateway.identity.stepUp.enabled: false`.

For OIDC step-up, your identity provider must return `auth_time` when Kurahi sends `max_age`
(Microsoft Entra ID, Okta, Google and Keycloak do).

Passkeys need an https origin (`gateway.identity.publicUrl` or `passkeys.allowedOrigins`; http only
for localhost) and a domain name, not an IP address. Everything else is off until you configure it.

## Roll back

`install.sh --version 2.4.0` (Windows: `install.ps1 -Version 2.4.0`) installs 2.4.0 again; see
[Updating](/install/updating#rollback). Before you start 2.4.0:

- **Remove `gateway.identity.passkeys`, `stepUp`, `nativeClients` and `deviceSignIn`** from the
  configuration: 2.4.0 does not accept keys it does not know.
- People who signed in only with passkeys or one-time codes cannot sign in on 2.4.0; use an
  identity provider or break-glass until you upgrade again. Their enrolled authenticators stay on
  disk and work again after the upgrade.
- Sign in again with `kurahi login` after you upgrade again; 2.4.0 has no command-line sign-in.

## Notes

- Kurahi Desktop 0.5.0 is unchanged and works with 2.5.0; native sign-in for Desktop follows in a
  Desktop update.
- Kurahi is self-hosted: your servers and any identity or model provider you choose are separate
  from Kurahi.
- Checksums for every asset are in `SHA256SUMS.txt` and `SHA256SUMS-desktop.txt`.
